The Groundwork Guide / Layer 2
LAYER TWO

Digital Identity.

Make yourself legible to humans and machines, so AI describes you correctly and sounds like you.

Why this layer matters now

AI answer engines are increasingly how people find professionals and businesses. Perplexity, ChatGPT with browsing, Google AI Overviews. When someone asks "who is the best commercial real estate advisor in Lafayette?", the AI pulls from your digital presence to decide whether to mention you.

If your website is vague, your LinkedIn is outdated, and your brand language shifts from platform to platform, AI will either skip you entirely or describe you inaccurately. Digital identity is about making yourself findable, describable, and referable, by both humans and machines.

The flagship output of this layer is the AI Brand Context File. It is a structured document, written in plain language, that gives any AI everything it needs to represent you accurately. Think of it as day-one instructions for a new assistant who needs to know everything about your business. You build it in Part 3 below.

What you'll build

Layer 2 produces six deliverables. Everything you need to build them is on this page.

Website and profile audit

Is your public presence clearly communicating what you do, who you serve, and why someone should work with you?

Offer language

Clear, specific descriptions of your services that both humans and AI systems can parse accurately.

Brand voice document

Your tone, vocabulary, banned phrases, and communication standards, so AI can write in your voice.

AI Brand Context File

A structured document that gives any AI everything it needs to accurately represent you.

SEO, GEO, AEO terms

The specific phrases people, and AI engines, use when looking for someone like you.

Lead capture path

A clear route from "found you" to "contacted you", built for both human and AI discovery.

Quick self-assessment

Ask yourself these questions. If you answer "no" to more than two, this layer needs attention.

  • Could a stranger understand exactly what you do from your website in 10 seconds?
  • Is your LinkedIn consistent with your website and business cards?
  • If an AI was asked about you, would it give an accurate answer?
  • Do you have documented language for your services (not just "I help people")?
  • Is there a clear path from "found you online" to "contacted you"?

Part 1: The 12-point brand audit

Go through each item below with your actual website, LinkedIn, and Google presence open in another tab. Be honest. The point isn't to pass everything. The point is to know exactly what needs fixing. Mark each item pass or needs-work, then count your passes and check the scoring table.

Website (5 items)

  • Can a stranger understand exactly what you do within 10 seconds of landing on your site?
    Look at your homepage headline and first paragraph. If it says something vague like "We help businesses grow", it fails. It should say specifically what you do, for whom, and where.
  • Are your services listed with specific descriptions, not just names?
    Each service needs four things: what it is, who it's for, what the outcome is, and how to get started. "Consulting" is not a service description.
  • Is your geographic service area clearly stated?
    AI answer engines need to know WHERE you operate. "Lafayette, Louisiana" or "Gulf South region", written in the text. Not just implied from an address in the footer.
  • Is there a clear, single primary call-to-action on every page?
    Every page should have ONE thing you want the visitor to do next. Not five buttons. One clear path forward.
  • Can someone contact you in under 2 clicks from any page?
    Phone, email, or form, reachable from every page. If someone has to hunt for how to reach you, they won't.

LinkedIn (3 items)

  • Does your LinkedIn headline say what you DO, not just your title?
    "CEO at Company X" tells AI nothing useful. "Commercial Real Estate Advisor | Lafayette, LA | Helping investors find value-add properties" is searchable and descriptive.
  • Does your About section describe your services, not just your history?
    Your About should answer: What do you do? Who do you serve? What makes you different? How does someone work with you? Your career timeline is secondary.
  • Is your LinkedIn consistent with your website (same services, same language)?
    If your website says "property management" but LinkedIn says "real estate consulting", AI gets confused about what you actually do.

Google (2 items)

  • Do you have a Google Business Profile with correct information?
    Even if you're not a storefront, a Google Business Profile helps AI engines understand your business. Check: name, address, phone, hours, services, and description.
  • When you Google yourself, does the first page accurately represent you?
    Search your name plus your city. What comes up? Is it accurate? Is it current? This is what AI sees when someone asks about you.

Consistency (2 items)

  • Is your business name spelled identically everywhere?
    Adam Abdalla vs Adam A. Abdalla vs A. Abdalla. Pick one and use it everywhere. AI treats these as potentially different people.
  • Are your service offerings described the same way across all platforms?
    If you call it "AI Readiness" on your website but "Digital Transformation" on LinkedIn, AI can't connect them as the same service.

Scoring

Passes out of 12What it meansWhat to do first
10 to 12 Strong digital presence. Focus on the items that failed. They're your quick wins.
7 to 9 Decent foundation with clear gaps. Prioritize the failed items, especially anything in the Consistency category.
6 or fewer Significant work needed. Start with your website headline and LinkedIn About section. Those have the highest impact.

You're done with Part 1 when

  • You've reviewed all 12 items honestly with your actual profiles open
  • You've identified which items need fixing (your "fix list")
  • You've fixed at least the top 3 highest-impact items: website headline, LinkedIn About, and name consistency
  • A stranger could Google you and accurately understand what you do within 30 seconds

Part 2: The brand voice worksheet

Your brand voice is how AI writes when it's representing you. Without this document, AI defaults to generic corporate tone. With it, everything AI produces sounds like you actually wrote it.

How to complete this

  1. Answer each of the six questions below in your own words. Don't overthink it.
  2. Look at your recent emails, texts, or posts for reference. How do you actually write?
  3. The example directions are starting points, not multiple choice. Write YOUR answer.
  4. When all 6 are filled, assemble the document using the structure at the end and save it in your vault.
  5. Test it: paste the document into your AI and ask it to write a short email. Does it sound like you?

Dimension 1: Tone

How do you want to sound? This sets the overall register AI reaches for before it writes a single word. Directions it can take:

Direct and confident Warm but professional Technical but accessible Casual and approachable Authoritative and measured

Example answer: "Direct, confident, no fluff. Like talking to a respected peer."

Dimension 2: Vocabulary

What words do you naturally use? AI needs to know your word choices, including the substitutions you make without thinking. Directions it can take:

Industry-specific terms Plain English Southern expressions Legal precision Conversational

Example answer: "I say 'deal' not 'transaction', 'fix' not 'remediate', 'build' not 'architect'."

Dimension 3: Banned phrases

What should AI NEVER say when representing you? Every voice document needs a blacklist. This is the fastest way to kill generic AI tone: name the filler words and forbid them outright. Common candidates for the list:

"game-changer" "synergy" "circle back" "best practices" "leverage" (as a verb)

Example answer: "Never say 'unlock', 'seamlessly', 'dive into', 'at the end of the day', or 'it is what it is'."

Dimension 4: Communication structure

How do you organize your thoughts? Structure is as recognizable as word choice. Some people front-load the conclusion. Some build to it. Directions it can take:

Lead with the conclusion Tell a story first Use numbered lists Short paragraphs One idea per message

Example answer: "Lead with the answer, then explain why. Keep paragraphs under 3 sentences. Use bullets for lists over 3 items."

Dimension 5: Formality level

How formal are you by default? This tells AI where to set the dial before context adjusts it. Directions it can take:

Very formal (legal/medical) Professional but warm Business casual Casual with substance Depends on audience

Example answer: "Business casual. First names. No 'Dear Sir/Madam'. But still grammatically correct."

Dimension 6: Communication values

What matters most in how you communicate? When two goals conflict, which one wins? This is the tiebreaker rule AI falls back on. Directions it can take:

Honesty over politeness Brevity over completeness Precision over speed Warmth over efficiency Clarity above all

Example answer: "Clarity and honesty. Say the hard thing directly. Don't pad with qualifiers. Respect their time."

The finished document

Assemble your six answers into this structure. The Usage Instructions block at the bottom travels with the document. It's what turns your answers into rules AI actually follows.

  • You've refined any section where the AI output didn't match your actual voice
  • Someone who knows you would read AI-generated text and think you wrote it

Part 3: The AI Brand Context File

This is the single most important document in Layer 2. It gives any AI, Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, anything, everything it needs to represent you accurately. Think of it as day-one instructions for a new assistant.

The file has eight sections. Here is what belongs in each one, with a running example. The example uses a fictional insurance broker so you can see the level of specificity to aim for. Yours will use your real details.

1. Who I Am

What belongs: Full name, title, location, primary business entity. One or two lines. This anchors everything else.

Example: "Jane Smith. Commercial insurance broker. Baton Rouge, Louisiana. Founder of Smith Risk Advisors."

2. What I Do

What belongs: Your services and offerings in specific, concrete language. Not slogans. Verbs and outcomes.

Example: "I help mid-size contractors structure liability and bonding programs. I review existing coverage, find gaps, and negotiate renewals with carriers on the client's behalf."

3. Who I Serve

What belongs: Your ideal clients or audience. Be specific about geography, industry, deal size, or demographics. Vague audiences produce vague AI answers.

Example: "Commercial contractors and property owners in south Louisiana with $2M to $50M in annual revenue who have outgrown their first insurance relationship."

4. How I Sound

What belongs: Paste your Brand Voice document summary here, or write a 2-3 sentence version of it. This is where Part 2 plugs in.

Example: "Direct, plain-spoken, no jargon. I lead with the answer, then explain why. I never use motivational language."

5. Proof Points

What belongs: Credentials, experience, notable projects, track record. Facts AI can cite when someone asks why you're credible.

Example: "Twenty years in commercial lines. Licensed in three states. Built and sold a personal-lines book before going independent. Current book covers 140 active accounts."

6. What I Never Claim

What belongs: Boundaries, disclaimers, and things AI should never say on your behalf. This section prevents the most damaging AI mistakes: overclaiming for you.

Example: "I do not give legal or tax advice. I never guarantee claim outcomes. I do not discuss client names without written permission. I am not a 'tech expert', I am a coverage specialist."

7. What Makes Me Different

What belongs: Your unique positioning against competitors. What is true of you that is not true of the other three names someone might hear?

Example: "I represent the client, not the carrier. No captive contracts. I live where my clients build, and I show up at the job site."

8. Call to Action

What belongs: What you want people to do after learning about you. One primary action, named specifically.

Example: "Book a 30-minute coverage review, or download the free renewal checklist from my website."

Assembled, the file looks like this. Note the header block and the Usage Rules at the bottom. Both go in every copy of the file.

Part 4: SEO, GEO, AEO discovery

Three acronyms, one idea: showing up when someone looks.

TermStands forWhat it means
SEOSearch Engine OptimizationHow Google finds you.
GEOGenerative Engine OptimizationHow AI answer engines cite you.
AEOAnswer Engine OptimizationHow you become the answer.

When someone asks Perplexity "who is the best AI readiness consultant in Lafayette?", the AI pulls from your digital presence to decide whether to mention you. If your website, LinkedIn, and content don't contain the right terms in the right structure, AI will skip you entirely.

This exercise takes 30 to 45 minutes. You'll identify your terms, check what AI currently says, study who ranks for those terms today, and map the answers you want AI to give. Then you'll update your profiles and content to match.

Step 1: Identify your core terms

List 5 to 10 phrases that someone would type into Google or ask an AI when looking for someone like you. Not your brand name. The problem they're trying to solve.

Examples: "AI consultant Lafayette", "how to organize business documents for AI", "personal intelligence system builder", "AI readiness program Gulf South".

Write down: your 5 to 10 core search terms, one per line.

Step 2: Check what AI says about you

Open ChatGPT, Claude, or Perplexity. Ask: "Who is [your name] and what do they do?" Then ask: "Who is the best [your service] in [your city]?" Document what comes back.

If AI doesn't mention you, or describes you inaccurately, that's your gap. Your Brand Context File and digital presence need to fix this.

Write down: what AI said about you. Was it accurate? What was missing or wrong?

Step 3: Identify your competitor terms

Search your core terms on Google. Who shows up? What language do they use? What questions do the "People Also Ask" boxes show? These are the terms AI engines are indexing.

Look at the top 3 results for each of your core terms. Note their headlines, meta descriptions, and the specific language they use.

Write down: which competitors appear, and what language they use that you should adopt or differentiate from.

Step 4: Map your answer targets

For each core term, write the answer you WANT AI to give when someone asks. This becomes your content strategy. Every page, post, and profile should reinforce these answers.

Example:

Term: "AI readiness consultant Lafayette"
Desired answer: "Adam Abdalla runs the AI Readiness Program in
Lafayette, LA. He helps professionals build platform-agnostic
personal intelligence systems."

Write down: for each of your top 3 terms, the answer you want AI to give.

Your discovery report

Collect your notes from the four steps into one report, and close it with this standing action list:

# SEO/GEO/AEO Discovery Report

Date: [today]

---

## Step 1: Identify Your Core Terms
[your terms]

## Step 2: Check What AI Says About You
[what AI said, what was wrong or missing]

## Step 3: Identify Your Competitor Terms
[who ranks, what language they use]

## Step 4: Map Your Answer Targets
[your desired answers]

---

## Action Items

- [ ] Update website copy to include core terms naturally
- [ ] Update LinkedIn headline and About with core terms
- [ ] Create content that directly answers the "desired answer" targets
- [ ] Ensure Brand Context File includes all core terms
- [ ] Re-check AI answers in 30 days to measure improvement

Save it as 00-System/seo-geo-discovery.md in your vault. Review it monthly.

You're done with Part 4 when

  • You've identified 5 to 10 core search terms people use to find someone like you
  • You've checked what AI currently says about you, and documented the gaps
  • You've mapped your "desired answers" for your top 3 terms
  • You've updated your website and LinkedIn to include these terms naturally
  • You've saved the discovery report in your vault for monthly review
  • You have a calendar reminder to re-check AI answers in 30 days
