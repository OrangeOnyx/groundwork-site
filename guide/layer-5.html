The Groundwork Guide / Layer 5
LAYER FIVE

Operations and Governance.

The operating agreement for your AI: access rules, verification habits, maintenance, and a digital estate plan.

Why this layer matters

AI is powerful. Power without rules is dangerous. Governance is not about limiting AI. It is about making it trustworthy. When you know exactly what AI can access, what it can do, and how to catch its mistakes, you can trust it with more. Clear boundaries lead to more useful AI, not less. That sounds backwards. It isn't.

Governance also means continuity. If something happens to you, your family or partners need to be able to access, understand, and maintain the system. That takes documentation, not just technology. This layer covers both: the rules that keep the system trustworthy while you run it, and the plan that keeps it alive if you can't.

Think of it like the operating agreement for a company. Nobody reads it on a good day. On a bad day, it is the only document that matters.

The five governance decisions

Your whole governance framework comes down to five decisions. Make each one, write it down, and you are governed. Skip them and you are guessing.

1. Access boundaries. What can AI see?

  • Categories AI can freely access (business documents, public records)
  • Categories with restrictions (financials, read-only)
  • Categories AI must never access (medical records, certain private matters)
  • Categories that require per-use permission

2. Authority levels. What can AI do?

  • Draft (emails, summaries). A human reviews before anything sends.
  • Execute (scheduling, filing). Only within defined parameters.
  • Recommend (decisions, strategies). A human decides.
  • Never act on (financial transactions, contractual commitments). No exceptions.

3. Verification protocol. How do we catch mistakes?

  • Spot-check process: ask AI for its sources, verify against the vault
  • Correction log: what went wrong, what was fixed
  • Monthly accuracy review
  • Escalation path for critical errors

4. Platform independence. Are we locked in?

  • Can the vault be read without any specific software? (Must be yes)
  • Is any information stored only in a proprietary format? (Must be no)
  • Could we switch LLM providers this week? (Must be yes)
  • Annual independence audit

5. Digital estate and continuity. What happens if something happens to you?

  • Designated successor or successors, with access instructions
  • Location of vault, credentials, and configurations documented
  • Instructions for maintaining versus archiving
  • Authority documentation (POA, trust provisions)

The rest of this page works through the three decisions that need the most detail: access boundaries, verification, and the estate plan. Authority levels were covered when you connected your AI in Layer 3, and platform independence is baked into how the vault is built. Both still get checked every month, as you'll see below.

Access boundaries: what AI may see and touch

Not everything in your vault should be equally accessible. Define clear boundaries by category so AI knows what it can use freely, what requires permission, and what it must never touch. This is one document, one page, and it removes a whole category of anxiety about connecting AI to your information.

The four access levels

Every category of information gets exactly one of these levels:

LevelWhat it means
FullAI can read, reference, quote, and include this in outputs.
Read-onlyAI can read this for context but must not include it in external outputs.
Permission-requiredAI must ask before accessing, every single time.
NeverAI must not access this under any circumstances.

Build your boundary table

List every major category of information in your vault. Assign each one a level and a note explaining the conditions. Here is a starting example:

CategoryAccess levelNotes
Business contractsFullAI can read and reference freely
Financial statementsRead-onlyAI can read but cannot share externally
Medical recordsNeverAI must never access or reference

Keep adding rows until every major category in your vault has a level. Aim for at least five categories. If a category doesn't fit one level cleanly, split it into two categories. "Financials" might become "historical financial statements" (read-only) and "current banking details" (never).

Write it down

Put the finished table in a markdown file and save it in your vault at 00-System/access-boundaries.md. Reference it from your governance rules so your AI setup actually reads it. The document looks like this:

# Access Boundaries

Last updated: 2026-08-14

| Category             | Access Level | Notes                                    |
|----------------------|--------------|------------------------------------------|
| Business contracts   | full         | AI can read and reference freely         |
| Financial statements | read-only    | AI can read but cannot share externally  |
| Medical records      | never        | AI must never access or reference        |

## Access Levels Defined

- **Full:** AI can read, reference, quote, and include in outputs
- **Read-only:** AI can read for context but must not include in external outputs
- **Permission-required:** AI must ask before accessing each time
- **Never:** AI must not access under any circumstances

Test it, don't trust it. A boundary that exists only on paper is a wish. Ask your AI about a "never access" category and confirm it refuses. Ask it to include "read-only" information in an outbound email and confirm it declines. If either test fails, your AI's instructions aren't actually pointing at the boundaries document. Fix that first.

You're done with access boundaries when:

  • Every major category of information in your vault has an access level
  • You've tested: asked AI about a "never access" category, and it correctly refused
  • You've tested: asked AI to include "read-only" information in an email, and it correctly declined
  • The document is saved in your vault and referenced in your governance rules

The verification protocol

Trust but verify. AI drifts. Information goes stale. Boundaries erode quietly. These spot-checks catch drift, stale information, and boundary violations before they become problems. Run them monthly. The whole routine takes about ten minutes.

The monthly spot-check (10 minutes)

Run each test below against your connected LLM. Mark pass or fail. Any failure means something in your vault needs updating. The failure is good news: you found the drift before it found you.

#Spot-checkWhat it tests
1Ask AI about a specific contract. Does it cite the correct version?Authority level awareness
2Ask AI to summarize a relationship. Does it match your entity file?Entity accuracy
3Ask AI to write in your voice. Does it match your Brand Voice doc?Voice consistency
4Ask AI about something private. Does it refuse to share externally?Access boundaries
5Ask AI about something historical. Does it note the date context?Temporal awareness

The correction log

For any failed check, document what went wrong and what you fixed. Two or three sentences is enough. A real entry looks like this:

"AI cited an expired lease as active. Fixed: moved lease to 02-Documents/historical/ and updated authority to 'historical'. Re-tested. Now correctly notes it's expired."

Notice the shape of that entry. It names the symptom, names the fix at the source, and confirms the re-test. That is the whole discipline in miniature: test, find drift, fix the source, re-test. You never fix the AI's answer. You fix the vault the answer came from. The AI is only ever as accurate as the source it reads.

Keep a verification log

Record each monthly session in a simple log and append it to 06-Maintenance/review-log.md. Use this format:

# Verification Log, 2026-08-14

## Spot-Check Results

1. Ask AI about a specific contract. Does it cite the correct version?
   Result: PASS
   Tests: Authority level awareness

2. Ask AI to summarize a relationship. Does it match your entity file?
   Result: PASS
   Tests: Entity accuracy

3. Ask AI to write in your voice. Does it match your Brand Voice doc?
   Result: FAIL
   Tests: Voice consistency

4. Ask AI about something private. Does it refuse to share externally?
   Result: PASS
   Tests: Access boundaries

5. Ask AI about something historical. Does it note the date context?
   Result: Not tested
   Tests: Temporal awareness

## Corrections Needed

AI drifted toward formal phrasing in drafts. Fixed: added two
recent email examples to the Brand Voice doc. Re-tested, matches.

## Next Review Date

2026-09-14

Over time this log becomes its own asset. Six months of entries tells you exactly where your system tends to drift and which sources need the most attention.

You're done with verification when:

  • You've run all 5 spot-checks against your live AI system
  • Any failures have been diagnosed and fixed, with the correction logged
  • The verification log is saved in your maintenance folder
  • You have a monthly calendar reminder to repeat this process
  • You understand the pattern: test, find drift, fix the source, re-test

The digital estate and continuity plan

What happens to your intelligence system if something happens to you? This is not morbid. It is responsible. Your family or partners need to be able to access, understand, and maintain the system. Without a plan, the vault you spent months building becomes a locked box nobody can open, on a computer nobody can log into, behind a subscription nobody knows exists.

The digital estate document

Create one document, stored securely and NOT in the vault itself, that covers five things:

1. Vault location

  • Physical location of the vault (which computer, which drive)
  • Backup locations (cloud drive, external drive)
  • How to open it (Obsidian, or any text editor)

2. Access credentials

  • Computer login
  • Cloud drive login
  • Any encryption passwords
  • AI subscription logins (Claude, ChatGPT, and any others)

3. Designated successor or successors

  • Who has authority to access the vault
  • Who makes decisions about maintaining versus archiving
  • Supporting documentation (POA, trust provisions, will reference)

4. Instructions for your successor

  • How to maintain the system (point them to the monthly checklist)
  • What to archive versus what to keep active
  • Who to contact for help (whoever advises you on your AI setup, if anyone)
  • What to do with private and sensitive sections

5. Platform independence verification

  • Confirm the vault is readable without any specific software
  • Confirm no information is stored only in proprietary formats
  • Confirm your successor can access everything without your biometrics

Important: where to store this document. Do NOT store it in your vault. It contains passwords, and your vault is exactly what your AI reads. Store it in a secure location your successor knows about: a safe, a sealed envelope kept with your estate documents, or a password manager shared with your designated person.

Then do the one step most people skip: tell your successor the document exists and where to find it. A perfect continuity plan nobody knows about is not a continuity plan. Walk them through it once. Better yet, have them open the vault on their own machine while you watch. That single rehearsal proves the whole chain works: they can find the document, use the credentials, reach the files, and read them with nothing but a text editor.

You're done with the estate plan when:

  • The digital estate document exists and covers all 5 sections
  • At least one designated successor knows the document exists and where to find it
  • You've verified: your successor could access the vault without your help
  • Estate documentation (POA or trust) references your digital assets, if applicable
  • You have a calendar reminder to review this document annually

Monthly maintenance (30 minutes)

Once your system is built, maintenance is minimal. One 30-minute session per month keeps everything accurate and useful. Put it on the calendar as a recurring block and run this sequence:

  1. Review the ingestion queue. Any new documents that need to be added?
  2. Check for stale information. Anything outdated that needs updating or archiving?
  3. Run the correction log. Did AI get anything wrong this month? Fix the source.
  4. Review automations. Are they still working correctly? Any new ones to add?
  5. Update preferences. Has anything changed about how you work or what you want?
  6. Platform independence check. Could you switch providers today if needed?

The ten-minute verification spot-check from this page fits inside step 3. Thirty minutes a month is the entire ongoing cost of the system. Compare that to the hours it hands back every week.

All five layers, complete

If you've done the work in this layer and the four before it, you now have a complete, governed, platform-agnostic personal intelligence system. It compounds daily. You own it outright. It will outlast any single AI provider. And with the estate plan in place, it will outlast you too.
