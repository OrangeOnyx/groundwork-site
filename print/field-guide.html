01 · Definition

What this is

Groundwork is not a promise. It is a folder of files. You build fifteen plain-text documents in a fixed order, and those documents become the foundation any AI can stand on.

Most teams skip this and go straight to agents. The result is generic output, invented policy, and systems that cannot tell doctrine from a draft. Groundwork installs the missing foundation: who you are, what your system may trust, what it may do, what survives failure, how you know it is working, and how it is maintained.

The sequence is not optional, because each order depends on the one before it. If your identity is unclear, your knowledge is unclassified, and your rules are unwritten, an agent will amplify the mess faster than you can catch it. The rule underneath everything: do not automate chaos.

The first three orders

Know me, Know my information, Know your limits. Ten files, required first. Output quality and decision boundaries improve immediately, before a single agent exists.

Order 4: one Operator

The scope written as file eleven, then a single scoped agent. Not autonomous. Not a swarm. It drafts, cites authority, and stops when it should.

The standing half

Remember and Improve: four documents, one loop (capture, understand, decide, act, verify, remember, improve), and the rhythm that keeps it true.

Multi-agent last

Optional. Only after the Operator is stable. Duplicate the folder pattern with a narrower charter. The human still decides who is invoked.

Everything here is free and ungated: this book, the website, and the open curriculum on GitHub. The files are yours, in plain text, on your machine. Markdown works with Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, or any future AI. No lock-in. Ever.

02 · Architecture

The six standing orders

Six standing orders, built in sequence. The first three are the foundation, the Operator activates at order four, and the last two never finish.

OrderNameWhat it settles
01 Know meIdentityWho you are and how you are allowed to behave.
02 Know my informationKnowledgeWhat the system is allowed to trust and use.
03 Know your limitsGovernanceWhat the system is allowed to do.
04 Help me operateThe OperatorOne scoped agent, activated on the foundation.
05 RememberContinuityWhat survives over time, people, and failure.
06 ImproveMeasurement & RhythmWhether it works, and how it stays true.

From activation on, the system also runs one loop: capture, understand, decide, act, verify, remember, improve. Chapters 07 and 08 cover the activation itself.

The test for every order: could a stranger read its documents and then write, decide, and refuse the way you would? If yes, the order is done. If no, keep sharpening.

03 · Sequence

The build path

You do not need to understand the whole system to start. You need one sitting, one folder, and one honest page.

First 30 minutes

Day one

Take the readiness scorecard and write down your weakest order. Read the four building blocks in Foundations on the site: the model is a well-read librarian, agents are its hands, prompts are instructions, and your documents are the fuel. Then create a folder called Groundwork, inside it 01_Identity, and write a rough Identity Charter. A rough draft counts. That is day one, done: you know your gap, you understand the machine, and the first of fifteen documents exists on your machine, owned by you.

Orders 1–3 · A focused weekend

Foundation install

Ten files. Output quality and decision boundaries improve immediately.

  • Identity Charter, Brand Voice, Decision Principles
  • Authority Classification, Context Vault, Strategic Priorities, Active Constraints
  • Automation Decision Framework, Escalation Rules, Allowed Tools
Order 4 · About a week

First Operator live

One scoped agent, running real work inside the foundation.

  • Write and lock the Agent Scope (file eleven)
  • Assemble the Operator context package
  • Run real Draft & Wait work through it
  • Correct the documents from actual misses
Orders 5–6 · Standing

The standing half

The foundation survives time, people, and drift.

  • Continuity & Succession
  • Session Notes discipline
  • Measurement Notes and the readiness scorecard

04 · Rules of engagement

Playbook

Your first working session

  1. Name the workspace after your business. One folder, plain text inside.
  2. Score the six orders out loud, honestly. Write notes on the gaps.
  3. Only the required documents come first. Resist building ahead.
  4. Write the Identity Charter in one honest sitting.
  5. If time remains, lock Brand Voice and Authority Classification.
  6. Do not promise yourself agents. Build a foundation the Operator can use.

When to activate the Operator

After the first ten files exist, a named human has signed off on the limits, and the Agent Scope (file eleven) is written. Load the package. Run one real Draft & Wait task. Then correct the documents.

Multi-agent

Do not build a swarm on a weak foundation. If a second role is later needed, duplicate the folder pattern with a narrower charter. The human decides who is invoked.

Do not

  • Invent policy
  • Treat drafts as doctrine
  • Add unauthorized tools
  • Promise autonomy
  • Skip escalation rules
05 · Scorecard

Readiness scorecard

Score each order 0 to 5, out loud, honestly. Notes go in the last column. The first three orders must be honest before an Operator is activated.

02 Know my informationCan the system tell doctrine from a draft?
03 Know your limitsDoes it know when to act, draft, or stop?
04 Help me operateIs one scoped Operator live, citing authority, stopping at zones?

07 · Order 4 · Activation

The Operator context package

One scoped agent, activated only after the first three orders exist. Not autonomous. Not a swarm. A competent internal operator that drafts, cites authority, and stops when it should.

When you are allowed to activate: files 1 through 10 exist, a named human has signed off on the limits, and file 11, the Agent Scope, describes one role in one sentence. If any of that is missing, the Operator will improvise it, and improvised policy is how automation goes wrong.

What an Operator is not: it is not autonomous, it does not act while you sleep, it does not have memory you have not given it, and it is not a fleet. It is one agent, one context package, one human in the loop. For most owners this is enough. Permanently.

Everything the Operator knows lives in one folder, loaded in order. This is the package: the fifteen Groundwork documents arranged for loading. A vault that contains everything contains nothing; this is the deliberate subset.

00_CONTEXT.md is a one-page index: what this package is, the load order, and the one-line rule: higher authority levels override lower ones. Load the files first, in order. Then paste the Operator prompt as the session instruction.

08 · Order 4 · The session instruction

The Operator prompt

This is the session instruction. Do not expand it into a personality. Keep it as rules.

The first two weeks

Activation is a shakedown cruise, not a launch party. Run it like this:

  • Days 1–3: Zone 1 only. Internal summaries, formatting, file work. You are testing whether the Operator loads context correctly and cites authority levels, not whether it can do your job.
  • Days 4–14: real Draft & Wait work. Give it the actual emails, notices, and reports on your desk. Approve, reject, and (this is the important part) when a draft misses, fix the document that caused the miss, not just the draft.
  • Log every correction in Session Notes. Three misses with the same root cause means a foundation file needs editing. That loop (miss, trace, edit, retest) is the whole method.
09 · Boundaries

Agent scope and multi-agent

The Operator may

  • Draft work inside Draft & Wait
  • Summarize Level 0–2 material
  • Flag missing or conflicting authority
  • Prepare recommended next actions

The Operator may not

  • Invent policy
  • Expand its own authority
  • Execute irreversible actions
  • Spawn additional agents without a human decision

Adding roles later

The default is one. One Operator, one context package, one human in the loop. Add a second role only after the Operator is stable and a second, clearly different job exists: a Researcher that never drafts client-facing work, or a Reviewer that only checks authority and voice.

How to add one: do not build an orchestration engine. Duplicate the folder pattern. Give the new role a narrower charter, its own Allowed Tools, and the same Escalation Rules. The human decides who is invoked.

What to refuse: agents that create other agents. Shared memory without classification. Any role that can execute Never Automate work.

The Operator is working when

  • Drafts arrive on-voice without re-prompting
  • Material claims come with authority levels attached
  • It refuses Zone 3 requests and escalates cleanly, stating what it needs
  • Corrections per week are falling because the documents keep improving
  • You trust it with a category of work you used to do at 11pm
